The NTK Method

Four questions.
One story.

Every story NTK publishes answers the same four questions, in the same order, from most certain to least. Once you know the shape, you can read any story in a minute and know exactly how sure to be about each part of it.

The origin

Thomas Jefferson
hated the news.
He also designed NTK.

He had his reasons. A pamphleteer Jefferson paid to attack John Adams had turned his pen on Jefferson, and by his second presidential term Jefferson decided newspapers were hopeless. So when a 17-year-old wrote and asked for reading and journalism career advice, Jefferson composed his famously cynical take:

"The man who never looks into a newspaper is better informed than he who reads them."

Then, in the same letter, he described the newspaper that might fix it. Four sections: Truths. Probabilities. Possibilities. Lies. (The first section would be the shortest.)

It was a historians' joke and, for more than 200 years, a footnote. Until now.
